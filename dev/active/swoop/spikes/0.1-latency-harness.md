## 2. The measurement contract

This is the part `research/review-1-latency.md` **F3** demands. Everything in §3 onwards is calibration of the
instrument this section defines.

### 2.1 Two numbers, never one

Every latency figure in the swoop project is one of exactly two kinds, and the kind is part of the number.

**Renderer-visible (`_rv`).** From a named stimulus to the moment the client's *renderer* can read the changed
pixels out of the surface it drew into. The probe reads the renderer's own buffer — `getImageData` on the
canvas it just filled, or `gl.readPixels` — and **never** `drawImage`s a `desynchronized` canvas into a scratch
canvas (review-1 F7; see §6, where that claim is tested on Chrome 153). It excludes the client's compositor, its
scanout and its panel.

**Photon (`_ph`).** The same stimulus to the moment the light actually changes on the client's panel. It can
only be measured with a camera or a photodiode. It is the number plan.md's success criteria are written
against.

The two are related by one additive constant:

```
X_ph  =  X_rv  +  C_photon
```

`C_photon` is defined in §5. Reporting an `_rv` number as if it were a product latency, or adding a guessed
`C_photon` to manufacture a photon number, is the specific failure F3 exists to prevent.

### 2.2 Real LAN hop versus same machine

**Same-machine (loopback) runs rank arms. They are never an absolute product latency.** The moonlight-web
26.8–30.3 ms figure the draft plan leaned on was a same-machine, renderer-visible, 720p→165 Hz, n = 10 number,
and its own source says to "treat them as ranking within a series, never as absolute latencies of the product"
(review-1 F3). Every same-machine row in this project carries the literal words *ranking only* next to it.

**Real LAN hop** means two physical machines on the same switch, the client's browser on one and
`owlette-swoop.exe` on the other, ICE resolving to a host-candidate pair (assert it from `getStats`'
selected-candidate-pair, do not assume it). This is the only configuration a product latency may be quoted
from. A WAN/impaired row states its impairment tool, version and parameters.

### 2.3 Sample size and statistics

- **n ≥ 100 per series**, and the series must span the phase of the display's refresh period rather than one
  point in it. Both halves of this harness dither their stimulus interval across a whole refresh period for
  exactly that reason (`PHASE_DITHER_MS = 17` in `flip.rs`; `setTimeout(40 + i % 17)` in `probe.js`). A series
  driven at a fixed 5 Hz on a 60 Hz panel samples one vsync phase 100 times and reports a distribution
  narrower than the real one.
- Report **p50 and p95**, plus `n`, min, max and sd. Nearest-rank percentiles (`stats::summarize`,
  `summarize()` in `probe.js` — the same definition on both sides).
- A series that contains a window that was ever hidden, or a run whose target window lost focus, is void.
- Report the **unresolved count** alongside any stage that can fail to resolve (see §4.3).

### 2.4 Separate rows for 60 Hz and ≥ 120 Hz clients

They are never pooled. The refresh period is the dominant quantum in every term after the encoder, so a
mixed-panel series has a bimodal distribution whose p50 means nothing.

- **60 Hz client row** — measured here. The client's refresh period is confirmed from the browser, not
  assumed: `?mode=selfflip` reports `rafPeriodContinuous`, which must sit at 16.6–16.8 ms for this row.
- **≥ 120 Hz client row** — **not measured. The hardware is not on this box**: both displays are
  3840x2160 @ 60 Hz (spike 0.8 §1/§3). The row is defined exactly as the 60 Hz row with the client on a
  ≥ 120 Hz panel and `rafPeriodContinuous` p50 ≤ 8.4 ms, and it stays in every table as an explicit
  **unmeasured** cell until the monitor in plan.md's "Start now" list arrives. It must not be dropped,
  interpolated from the 60 Hz row, or estimated from "three of the four fixed stages halve".

### 2.5 The QPC ↔ `performance.now()` offset

Host stage timestamps are `QueryPerformanceCounter` ticks; browser stage timestamps are `performance.now()`
milliseconds. They share no epoch, so without this step every per-stage number in the stats overlay is "a guess
with a plausible-looking decimal point" (`research/review-3-delivery.md` **F12**). The method, which is what
F12 asks this memo to state:

**Four-timestamp NTP exchange.** For each exchange the browser records `t0` before the request and `t3` when the
response resolves; the host records `t1` when it has read the request and `t2` immediately before it writes the
response, both as QPC ticks converted to milliseconds by the host's own `QueryPerformanceFrequency`.

```
offset = ((t1 - t0) + (t2 - t3)) / 2        so   qpc_ms  ≈  performance.now() + offset
delay  =  (t3 - t0) - (t2 - t1)             round trip with the server's own residence removed
```

Rules:

1. **Take the offset of the minimum-`delay` exchange**, not the mean of all of them (NTP's own filter). Its
   error is bounded by ±`delay`/2, and that bound is published with the offset.
2. **Burst the exchanges back to back.** Spreading them out inflates `delay` and therefore the uncertainty: on
   this box a back-to-back series gave ±0.199 ms and a series spread over 120 s gave ±0.398 ms (§4.4).
3. **n ≥ 100 exchanges**; 300–400 costs a quarter of a second.
4. The host must timestamp **as close to the wire as the language allows**. `latency-target`'s endpoint is
   std-only for this reason; its measured residence `t2 - t1` is 3.3 µs p50 (n = 400).
5. **Measure once per session and re-check once at the end.** Measured drift is 1.31 ppm over 117.4 s (§4.4),
   which is below the estimator's own uncertainty, so a mid-session re-measure is not needed; a re-check at the
   end is cheap and catches a suspend/resume.
6. Every per-stage figure derived through the offset is quoted **no finer than the offset's uncertainty**.
   ±0.2 ms is the floor on this box; a figure written to 0.01 ms is false precision.
7. In the product (Task 4.7 / D16) the same exchange runs over the **control channel**, not over HTTP, with the
   host's QPC ticks in the reply. The arithmetic is unchanged.

### 2.6 What the client's `performance.now()` can resolve

Measured at **0.1000 ms** on Chrome 153 without cross-origin isolation (n = 200,000 back-to-back reads, every
mode records it). Browser-side stage numbers are quantised by it. It is recorded in the metadata of every probe
run so a future browser's coarser clamp cannot silently widen a distribution.

## 3. What the instrument is

**`latency-target`** (Rust, Win32): a `WH_MOUSE_LL` hook installed on the message-pump thread, a borderless
fullscreen window on the chosen monitor backed by a **DXGI flip-model swapchain**, and four QPC ticks per
left-button-down — in the hook, at message dispatch, around `Present` — appended to a CSV.

The swapchain is the part that is not obvious and it is what makes §5 possible.
`IDXGISwapChain::GetFrameStatistics` returns `SyncQPCTime`, the QPC of the **vertical blank at which a present
was displayed**. That converts "the compositor wait" from a modelled term into a measured one, and leaves only
scanout and panel response invisible to the process. Frame statistics are only implemented for the flip model,
which is why the window is not a GDI `FillRect`.

The hook does no work: it reads QPC, stores it into a preallocated lock-free ring and posts the slot index. A
`WH_MOUSE_LL` callback that blocks is removed by the OS after `LowLevelHooksTimeout`, and a callback that waits
on a lock held by the UI thread would also time-shift the very quantity being measured. For the same reason the
pump never sleeps: it uses `MsgWaitForMultipleObjectsEx(…, 4 ms, QS_ALLINPUT, MWMO_INPUTAVAILABLE)`, a message
wait state in which low-level hooks still run, and resolves pending frame statistics on the timeout.

`--autoclick N` injects left clicks with `SendInput` so a series of 190 reaches `n ≥ 100` without a human;
`LLMHF_INJECTED` is recorded per sample so an injected series can never be confused with a physical one. A
physical mouse adds a device and driver ingress term *before* the hook that this harness cannot see at all
(§5.4).

**`latency-probe-web`** (static page + a ~110-line node server, no dependencies and no `package.json`): four
modes — `clock`, `readback`, `selfflip`, `clickflip` — each of which computes its own p50/p95 and POSTs the
whole run as JSON to the server, which writes it to `runs/`. Nothing in this harness requires a human to read a
number off a screen.

Neither half implements any pipeline: no capture, no encode, no transport, no decode. That is 0.2's job, and
0.2 may copy either of these.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hook → message dispatch | 190 | 0.10 | **0.13** | 0.15 | 0.16 | 0.20 | 0.21 | 0.13 | 0.02 |
| dispatch → `Present` call | 190 | 0.01 | **0.01** | 0.01 | 0.02 | 0.02 | 0.02 | 0.01 | 0.00 |
| `Present` call → return | 190 | 0.11 | **0.14** | 0.20 | 0.22 | 0.28 | 0.29 | 0.15 | 0.03 |
| **hook → `Present` returned** | 190 | 0.24 | **0.28** | 0.36 | 0.40 | 0.45 | 0.45 | 0.29 | 0.05 |
| `Present` call → vblank displayed | 190 | 2.21 | **10.07** | 17.27 | 18.29 | 19.53 | 28.04 | 10.69 | 4.81 |

All milliseconds. Raw: `latency-target/runs/host-60hz-mon0.csv`.

Two things worth keeping:

- **The application's own work is 0.28 ms p50.** Everything above 0.3 ms in a host-local click-to-flip number
  on this box is the compositor, not the code.
- **The compositor wait is not half a refresh period.** The naive model (present times uncorrelated with vsync
  → uniform 0…16.66 ms → mean 8.33) predicts less than what was measured: mean **10.69 ms**, and
  `Present → vblank` reaches 28.04 ms. Bucketed by whole refresh periods, **161 of 190 (84.7 %)** flips were
  displayed within one refresh period of the click and **29 of 190 (15.3 %)** took two. A present that lands
  inside the compose/flip-queue deadline just before a vblank waits for the *next* one. Any budget that carries
  "half a frame" for this term under-counts it by ~2.4 ms at the mean and by a whole frame 15 % of the time.

### 4.2 Host: the same run on monitor 1 (rotated 4K, 125% scaling)

`flip --monitor 1 --seconds 42 --autoclick 5`, **n = 190**, and a finding: **195 of 200 presents never
resolved frame statistics.**

| stage | n | p50 | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| hook → `Present` returned | 190 | 0.26 | 0.35 |
| `Present` call → vblank displayed | **5** | 23.48 | 31.77 |
| hook → vblank displayed | **5** | 23.69 | 31.88 |

The application half is identical to monitor 0 — a 2160x3840 fullscreen swapchain on a rotated, 125 %-scaled
output costs nothing measurable in `Present`. But `GetFrameStatistics` resolved only 5 of 200 presents there,
against 200 of 200 on the primary, and all five of those were in the second refresh period.

*Inference, not measurement:* a borderless fullscreen flip-model swapchain on the primary gets independent
flip, in which the swapchain is handed straight to the display and its `PresentCount` tracks our presents
exactly; a **rotated** output cannot take that path because rotation needs a compose pass, so the swapchain is
composed by DWM and its statistics no longer line up one-to-one with our presents.

**Consequence for 0.2 and for the camera pass: run the host side on monitor 0.** Frame statistics are the
mechanism that makes the compositor term measurable, and on this box they only work there.

### 4.3 The frame-statistics resolution rule

`GetFrameStatistics` describes the most recently *displayed* present, so the harness only accepts
`SyncQPCTime` when `PresentCount` equals the sample's own `GetLastPresentCount`. A sample whose count has
already been passed is left **unresolved** and counted, rather than given a neighbouring frame's vblank. That
is why §4.2 can report "5 of 200" instead of 200 plausible numbers. Any code that reads frame statistics —
including 0.2's — must keep that exact-match rule.

### 4.4 QPC ↔ `performance.now()` offset

Chrome 153, `?mode=clock`, against `latency-target clock` on 127.0.0.1:17431.

| run | n | offset (ms) | uncertainty | delay p50 | delay p95 | server residence p50 | offset sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| back to back | 400 | 1 773 863 009.4029 | **± 0.1989** | 0.5974 | 0.7971 | 0.0033 | 0.0510 |
| spread over 120 s | 300 | 1 773 885 790.9402 | ± 0.3980 | 1.1935 | 1.8882 | 0.0065 | — |

`qpc_ms ≈ performance.now() + offset`. The two offsets are from different page loads (each
`performance.timeOrigin` is its own epoch) and are not comparable to each other; what is comparable is the
*quality* of each estimate.

- **Precision: ±0.199 ms** from a back-to-back burst of 400 exchanges, and the offset's own sd across the
  series is 0.051 ms. That is the floor on how finely any cross-clock stage may be quoted (§2.5 rule 6).
- **Drift: 0.1538 ms over 117.4 s = 1.31 ppm**, which is *smaller than the ±0.398 ms uncertainty of the
  estimate it was computed from*, so it is noise, not resolvable drift. Both clocks derive from the same
  hardware counter. **Measure the offset once per session.**
- **The server costs 3.3 µs p50** (`t2 - t1`, n = 400), so the four-timestamp form is removing a real but tiny
  term; the dominant uncertainty is the loopback round trip.
- The exchange works cross-origin from a `127.0.0.1:17430` page to `127.0.0.1:17431` with
  `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`; Chrome 153's local-network restrictions did not interfere (n = 700
  exchanges across the two runs, zero failures).

### 4.5 Browser: renderer-visible instrument overhead

`?mode=selfflip&n=200`, Chrome 153, 1264x821 CSS px, dpr 1, window never hidden, 0 wrong pixels in 200 flips.

| series | n | min | p50 | p90 | p95 | p99 | max | mean | sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| decide → `fillRect` returned | 200 | 0.00 | **0.00** | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 3.20 | 0.04 | 0.23 |
| **decide → pixels readable** | 200 | 0.20 | **0.30** | 0.50 | 0.50 | 0.60 | 3.70 | 0.38 | 0.25 |
| decide → next rAF callback | 200 | 0.40 | 1.00 | 6.70 | 8.10 | 10.30 | 15.30 | 2.72 | 2.78 |
| decide → rAF `timestamp` | 200 | **-16.10** | 0.40 | 6.50 | 7.90 | 10.10 | 15.00 | -0.78 | 6.72 |
| **rAF period, continuous** | 180 | 16.50 | **16.70** | 16.80 | 16.80 | 16.80 | 66.70 | 16.94 | 3.72 |
| `getImageData` reads per flip | 200 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.00 |

- **The probe adds 0.30 ms p50 / 0.50 ms p95** between deciding to draw and being able to read the pixels back.
  A single `getImageData` was always enough (1.00 reads per flip, n = 200). This is the instrument's own
  overhead and it is inside every `_rv` number this harness produces.
- **The browser sees 16.70 ms p50 (n = 180)**, matching spike 0.8's 16.66 ms `AcquireNextFrame` interval
  measured through an entirely different path. The 60 Hz row's label is confirmed, not assumed.
- **rAF's `timestamp` is not a present time and must never be used as one.** It went as far as **16.1 ms
  negative** relative to a draw that preceded the callback, because it reports the start of the frame the
  callback runs in, which can precede the request. This is direct support for plan.md D17: present from the
  decoder's output callback, and use `requestVideoFrameCallback` only to *measure*.
- The "decide → next rAF callback" row is **not** the general rAF quantisation. The probe's `setTimeout`
  wake-ups turned out to be correlated with the frame boundary, which pulls that p50 down to 1.00 ms. The
  honest general statement is arithmetic on the measured period: an rAF-scheduled *sampler* observes an event
  arriving at a time uncorrelated with the frame boundary U(0, 16.70 ms) later — **mean 8.35 ms, p95 15.9 ms**
  of pure quantisation, added to whatever it is sampling. That is the term the moonlight-web numbers in
  review-1 F3 carry, and the reason the presentation contract forbids rAF scheduling.

## 5. `C_photon` — the term the harness cannot see

### 5.1 Definition

```
C_photon  =  (photon time of the sampled region on the client panel)
           - (the renderer-visible timestamp of the same flip)
```

It is a property of the **client's** display chain only. The *host's* compositor wait is **not** part of it: in
the product, Desktop Duplication delivers frames at the compositor's cadence, so the host compositor sits
*inside* the measured pipeline (0.2 timestamps it) and the host's panel is never in the path at all.

### 5.2 What is known today

| component | value | status |
|---|---|---|
| client compositor wait (fullscreen flip-model surface, 60 Hz, this box) | p50 **10.07** ms, mean **10.69**, p95 **18.29**, n = 190 | **measured** on an equivalent surface (§4.1) — a proxy for Chrome's, not a measurement of Chrome |
| display refresh period | **16.70** ms p50, n = 180 (browser); 16.66 ms p50, n = 900 (spike 0.8) | measured, twice, by different paths |
| scanout to the sampled region | 0 ms at the top of the frame → 16.66 ms at the bottom; **8.33 ms at the centre** | derived from the measured period, not itself measured |
| panel processing, pixel response, backlight | — | **unmeasured** — this is what the camera is for |

### 5.3 What is published, and the rule that goes with it

> **`C_photon(60 Hz, this box) = 10.1 ms (compositor, measured proxy) + S`**, where
> **`S` = scanout + panel = PENDING [human]**, to be measured by the protocol in §7.

Until `S` exists:

1. Every latency figure in the swoop project is published as `X_rv` with its `n`.
2. A photon figure may be written as `X_rv + C_photon` **only** with `S` named as unmeasured in the same
   sentence. **No one adds a guessed `S`.** A number like "p50 ≈ 48 ms photon" is forbidden until the camera
   pass returns.
3. `C_photon` is a single project-wide constant, so rankings between arms in `_rv` terms are identical to
   rankings in `_ph` terms. That is the whole point of fixing it: **the G1 decision does not need the camera.**
4. When the camera pass lands it measures the *total* directly, which supersedes the decomposition above; the
   decomposition only exists so the total can be sanity-checked against it.
5. Translating plan.md's criteria: the 60 Hz client criterion `p50_ph ≤ 55 ms` becomes
   **`p50_rv ≤ 44.9 − S` ms**, and `p95_ph ≤ 80 ms` becomes `p95_rv ≤ 61.7 − S` ms using the p95 compositor
   term. With `S` plausibly a few milliseconds to ~20 ms on a 4K60 panel, an arm whose `p50_rv` is under ~30 ms
   is comfortably inside the criterion and an arm over ~45 ms is outside it regardless of `S`. Arms landing
   between those two need the camera before anyone claims a pass.

### 5.4 What is outside `C_photon` and outside this harness entirely

The **input device's own ingress**: the switch bounce, the mouse's 1000 Hz (or 125 Hz) USB polling interval,
and the HID stack, all of which happen before either the low-level hook or the browser's pointer event. The
harness's injected clicks bypass it by construction. It is inside a camera-measured click-to-photon number and
must not be subtracted out of one; it is a real part of what a user feels, and it belongs to the *client*
machine, not to swoop.

## 6. review-1 F7 tested: does a `desynchronized` canvas read back empty?

F7 records that moonlight-web's probe found a canvas presented with `desynchronized: true` "reads back empty
through `drawImage`". Every telemetry decision downstream depends on that, so it was tested rather than
inherited. `?mode=readback`, **24 configurations** × 60 reads each, expected pixel `255,0,0,255`.

**It did not reproduce. All 24 configurations returned the correct pixel on Chrome 153 on this box** —
including an in-DOM, `desynchronized: true`, *presented* canvas read through `drawImage` into a scratch canvas.

Readback cost, p50 / p95 milliseconds, the rows that matter:

| configuration | p50 | p95 | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| in-DOM, presented, 2d `desynchronized:true`, `getImageData` | 1.10 | 2.30 | 2.80 |
| in-DOM, presented, 2d `desynchronized:false`, `getImageData` | **0.00** | 0.10 | 2.30 |
| in-DOM, presented, 2d `desynchronized:true`, `drawImage` → `getImageData` | 2.30 | 2.50 | 2.90 |
| in-DOM, presented, webgl2 `desynchronized:true`, `readPixels` | 2.20 | 2.40 | 2.90 |
| in-DOM, not presented, 2d `desynchronized:true`, `getImageData` | 0.60 | 1.10 | 3.20 |
| detached, 2d `desynchronized:true`, `getImageData` | 0.70 | 4.90 | **62.30** |

Conclusions, in order of how load-bearing they are:

1. **Keep the rule, drop the reason.** The contract (§2.1) still says to read the renderer's own buffer, but
   the reason is now *cost*, not emptiness: a `desynchronized` canvas costs 0.6–2.3 ms p50 per read against
   0.00 ms for a synchronized one, and `drawImage` adds another ~1 ms on top. In a harness whose whole budget
   is tens of milliseconds, a 2.3 ms readback is not free.
2. **This is one browser on one box.** Chrome 153 / Windows 11 23H2 / RTX 2080 Ti / driver 591.86. F7's claim
   may well hold on the hardware moonlight-web measured. Task 0.10 (web presentation probe) covers Safari and
   Firefox and should re-run `?mode=readback` there; **a probe must still verify its own readback before it
   trusts a series**, which is why the mode exists and why `selfflip` and `clickflip` both count wrong pixels
   (0 of 200 and 0 of 150 here).
3. **The `desynchronized` hint is advisory and this run cannot prove Chrome honoured it.** The ~0.6–2.3 ms
   readback penalty against 0.00 ms for `desynchronized:false` is indirect evidence that a different path was
   taken, but it is evidence, not proof.
4. The 62.30 ms max on a *detached* `desynchronized` canvas is an artefact of a configuration nobody ships;
   it is listed only so the in-DOM rows are not read as if they were the same thing.

### 7.1 Phone settings, and the one that ruins the run

- **240 fps or better.** iPhone: Settings → Camera → Record Slo-mo → **1080p at 240 fps**, then shoot in
  **Slo-mo** mode. Android: use the plain **"Slow motion" 240 fps** mode.
- **Do not use "Super Slow-mo" / 960 fps / any "AI" or "motion-boost" mode.** They capture a short interpolated
  burst; interpolated frames are invented and will produce confident, wrong numbers.
- **Record the actual frame rate from the file, not from the menu.** Phones routinely write a 240 fps track
  into a 30 fps container. Read it back:
  `ffprobe -v error -select_streams v:0 -show_entries stream=r_frame_rate,avg_frame_rate,nb_frames -of default=nw=1 clip.mov`
  and use `r_frame_rate` in every conversion. **State the rate next to every figure.**
- **Lock exposure and focus** before recording (iPhone: tap and hold until AE/AF LOCK). The flip is
  full-black ↔ full-white and will otherwise make the camera hunt.
- **Dim the room** and set the monitor to a moderate brightness, so the screen is the light source but the
  white frame does not bloom over the mouse.
- Phone on a tripod, ~1 m back, both subjects in focus, screen filling a good part of the frame.
- **Stick a small piece of bright tape on the top of the mouse button.** It turns the button's travel into a
  luminance change in a second small region, which makes the whole count automatable (§7.4) instead of 100
  frame-by-frame judgement calls.

### 7.2 P1 — host-local input → photon (this spike's own pending row)

What it closes: `S`, and the device-ingress term, as one measured total.

```
cd agent/swoop/spikes/latency-target
cargo run --release -- flip --monitor 0 --seconds 300 --autoclick 0 --csv runs/photon-host.csv
```

In frame: **the mouse button (taped) and monitor 0**. Nothing else needs to be visible.

Click **at least 100 times**, at least 1 s apart, deliberately *irregularly* — do not click to a metronome, or
the series samples one vsync phase (§2.3). Press Esc, or let the 300 s deadline end it; the CSV and the stdout
summary are written on exit.

Per trial: `T_input_to_photon = (f_flip - f_click) × 1000 / fps`. Report p50 and p95 over n ≥ 100, with fps and
the ±1-frame quantisation (±4.17 ms at 240 fps).

Then, using the same run's CSV (`hook → vblank displayed`, p50 10.22 ms here):

```
device_ingress + S  =  T_input_to_photon(p50)  -  (hook → vblank displayed)(p50)
```

Publish that as the sum. Splitting it further needs a second instrument (a photodiode on the mouse switch) and
is not worth it: `S` alone is only interesting as a component of `C_photon`, and `C_photon` is measured
directly by P3.

### 7.3 P2 and P3 — the rows 0.2 needs

**P3 is the product number and the one plan.md's success criteria are written against.**

**P3 — client input → client photon.** In frame: the mouse the operator is using **on the client machine**, and
the **client** monitor. The operator clicks inside the swoop page; the click travels to the host, the host's
test pattern flips, and the flip comes back. Count button-down → client screen change. This contains no unknown
constant and no host panel. n ≥ 100, p50 and p95, per arm.

**P2 — host photon → client photon**, the "both monitors in one frame" shot. Useful as a *diagnostic split* of
the return path (capture → encode → transport → decode → present), and it is much easier to automate than P3
because both events are screen luminance changes. But note the systematic error before quoting it as a product
number: **the host's panel is not in swoop's path.** Desktop Duplication captures at the compositor, well before
the host's scanout and panel. So P2 over-counts by the host panel's own delay, and if the two monitors are
different models that error does not cancel. Use identical panels where possible, state the models, and treat
P2 as a *ranking and diagnostic* row, never as the product criterion.

Both need the QPC↔`performance.now()` offset from §2.5 captured in the **same session**, so the camera-derived
totals can be reconciled against the in-harness per-stage breakdown.

### 7.4 Counting frames without counting frames

```
ffmpeg -i clip.mov -vsync 0 -q:v 2 frames/%06d.png
```

Then threshold the mean luminance of two fixed rectangles per frame — one over the taped mouse button, one over
a **small region near the top of the screen** — and take the first frame in each trial where each crosses its
midpoint. Sampling near the top of the screen keeps both the panel's scanout phase and the phone's rolling
shutter phase roughly constant across trials; state the region you used.

`Δframes × 1000 / fps` is the number. **Quantisation is ±1 frame — ±4.17 ms at 240 fps, ±8.33 ms at 120 fps.**
120 fps is half a refresh period on a 60 Hz panel and is too coarse for this work; if 240 fps is not available,
say so and treat the row as indicative.

Sanity checks that must pass before a series is used: no negative trial, and no trial beyond ~5 refresh periods
without an explanation.

## 8. Recommendation — the rows the G1 memo must report

G1 chooses between video paths A, B and C (plan.md D3) and between transport libraries (D4). The rule is
relative — the winner must beat B by ≥ 15 ms p50 on LAN and must not lose to B under impairment — so **the
decision does not need the camera**, and 0.2 must not be blocked on it. Concretely, the G1 memo reports:

**Required, measured by 0.2 itself, every cell with its `n` (≥ 100) and both p50 and p95:**

1. **`_rv`, real LAN hop, 1080p60, 60 Hz client, per arm (A / B / C)** — the row the D3 ≥ 15 ms margin is
   evaluated on, and the row plan.md's criteria are translated onto via §5.3 item 5. This is the most important
   row in the memo.
2. **`_rv`, real LAN hop, impaired (2 % loss, 40 ms RTT, 10 ms jitter), per arm** — with the impairment tool,
   its version and its exact parameters named. D3's second clause ("does not lose to B") is decided here.
3. **`_rv`, same machine, 1080p60, per arm** — labelled **ranking only** in the table itself, so it can never
   be quoted as a product latency (§2.2). It exists to be comparable with the moonlight-web numbers the plan
   cites, and for nothing else.
4. **Per-stage breakdown** for the winner and for B: capture → encode → send → arrival → decode → renderer
   visible, p50 and p95, host stages in QPC and browser stages in `performance.now()`, **reconciled through the
   offset of §2.5** — and the memo states that offset, its `n` and its ± uncertainty, and quotes no stage more
   finely than that uncertainty (±0.2 ms on this box).
5. **`rafPeriodContinuous` p50 for the client of every row**, as the evidence that a row labelled "60 Hz" was a
   60 Hz client.
6. **The `C_photon` line as published in §5.3**, copied verbatim, immediately under the first latency table.

**Required, camera pass, [human] — two arms only:**

7. **`_ph` by protocol P3, real LAN hop, 1080p60, 60 Hz client**, for **the winner and for path B**. Two arms,
   not three: the camera pass is the expensive part and B is the baseline the rule is written against. n ≥ 100,
   p50 and p95, with the capture frame rate and the ±1-frame quantisation stated. Until it runs, the cells read
   **PENDING [human] — protocol 0.1 §7.3**.
8. Optionally **P2** as a diagnostic split of the return path, carrying the host-panel caveat from §7.3.

**Required, and required to stay empty:**

9. **The ≥ 120 Hz client row**, for every arm, reading **unmeasured — no ≥ 120 Hz display on this box**
   (§2.4). It is not dropped, not interpolated from the 60 Hz row, and not estimated.

**Must not appear:**

10. Any photon number produced by adding a guessed `S` to an `_rv` number (§5.3 item 2).
11. Any absolute product latency taken from a same-machine row (§2.2).
12. Any pooled 60 Hz / ≥ 120 Hz figure (§2.4).

**Two operational notes for 0.2, both bought by this spike:** run the host side on **monitor 0**, because frame
statistics only resolve there on this box (§4.2), and keep the **exact-match** rule when reading them (§4.3);
and verify the probe's own readback with `?mode=readback` on every browser before trusting a series from it
(§6 item 2).

## 9. What this spike did not measure

- **Every photon number.** No camera or photodiode was used. §7 is a protocol, not a result.
- **The ≥ 120 Hz client row.** The hardware is not on this box.
- **Any pipeline term**: capture, encode, transport, decode and presentation are 0.2's, deliberately.
- **A physical mouse.** Every click in this spike — 400 host-side across the two monitor runs, 155 into the
  browser — was injected with `SendInput`, which excludes the device and driver ingress term (§5.4).
- **Chrome's actual compositor wait.** §4.1's 10.07 ms is measured on an equivalent fullscreen flip-model
  surface on the same display, not inside the browser.
- **Whether Chrome honoured `desynchronized: true`** (§6 item 3).
- **Any browser but Chrome 153**, and any OS but Windows 11 23H2. Task 0.10 covers Safari and Firefox.
- **Monitor 1's compositor wait** beyond n = 5 (§4.2).

## 10. Hand-off notes

- Subcommands and their exact invocations are in the crate-level doc comment in
  `latency-target/src/main.rs` and in the header comment of `latency-probe-web/server.mjs` and `public/probe.js`.
- `latency-target` changes exactly two pieces of system state, both restored: the cursor position, and — only
  with `--autoclick` — injected left clicks into its own topmost fullscreen window. A watchdog force-exits the
  process 10 s past the deadline so a wedged message pump can never leave that window owning the screen. Esc
  ends a run early.
- The clock endpoint answers on **127.0.0.1:17431** in both `flip` and `clock` modes; the probe page defaults to
  that port and a unit test pins the two together.
- `latency-probe-web` has **no `package.json` and no dependencies**; `node server.mjs` is the whole thing.
  `runs/` is gitignored on both sides.
- **For Task 1.2**: like `capture-probe` and `nvenc-probe`, this spike carries its own `Cargo.toml` with an
  empty `[workspace]` table and sits under `agent/swoop/spikes/`, so `agent/swoop`'s package needs
  `exclude = ["spikes"]` (or equivalent) to keep the nested manifests out of the product crate.
