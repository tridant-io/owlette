# A site's screens and changes belong to its members, and its screenshots stay until the site lets them go

The owner's rulings of 2026-11-26: nothing deletes a screenshot by age or count; a site owner or admin deletes it, or it goes when its machine is removed or moved off the site, or its site is deleted. A superadmin holds every capability on every site and reaches each one without a member row. Where they hold no row they may read the site (its machines, status, metrics, logs, deployments and settings) but never its screens, and change only an allowlist: update owlette on its machines, reboot a machine or cancel or dismiss a reboot, start, stop, restart or kill a configured process, cancel a deployment, revoke an agent token, and kill or kick a swoop session ("allowlist, refuse the rest"; the last three added on the owner's ruling of 2026-11-27). Every other change, from pairing or moving a machine to fleet grants, talons, processes, deployments, roost (a new version, a rollout, a rollback or a re-sync), presets, settings, members, webhooks and alerts, answers 403 `members_only` until they add themselves to the site through the platform's assign-sites, which the site's audit log records. Live view, the screenshot slideshow, is gone in favour of swoop. We chose a member-row test (`isSiteMember`, `isMemberRole`, not a capability) over taking capabilities from the superadmin role, because superadmins still manage every site from the platform, and we enforce it by default in the shared site gates rather than route by route, because a new route forgets a check and a gate does not. Screenshot records hold a path and an image-route url, and the image route is the only reader of the image itself.

## Consequences

- `authorizedSiteHandler`, `requireSiteAuthAndScope`, `requireMachineAuthAndScope` and `requireRoostAuthAndScope` refuse every request but GET, HEAD and OPTIONS from a person without a member row (`refusesNonMemberChange`), as part of site access, so the kill switch never turns it off. Ten routes admit them (`admitsNonMembers`): the commands route and the four process verbs, where `executeMachineCommand` queues only `update_owlette`, `reboot_machine`, `cancel_reboot`, `dismiss_reboot_pending`, `start_process`, `stop_process`, `restart_process`, `kill_process`, a tier-1 `mcp_tool_call` other than `capture_screenshot`, and `cancel_mcp_tool` (`nonMemberCommandRefusal`; the agent acts on a process only when its config names it); dismissing a pending reboot; cancelling a deployment; revoking an agent token; and killing or kicking a swoop session. The dashboard hides or locks every other change control for them, with one hint and a link to add themselves (`useMemberLock`). Pairing a machine and a move's target site (`assertUserHasSiteCapability`) and hoot's key provisioning refuse them the same way, which narrows ADR 0020: a superadmin moves a machine only between two sites they are a member of, since a move is a change to both. `__tests__/api/nonMemberChanges.test.ts` calls every write route under `app/api/sites/[siteId]/` as such a superadmin and fails on any that answers otherwise, and on an allowlist entry with no route behind it.
- Every platform path that makes someone a member or owner of a site writes a row in that site's own audit log, beside the platform's: assign-sites (`member_added`) and an account delete's handover to a successor (`ownership_transferred`).
- Hoot gives them tier 1 on that site, without `capture_screenshot`, and keeps them off local hoot. A hoot turn whose caller says nothing of their standing counts as no member.
- `requireMachineAuthAndScope` says `seesScreens`: true for the machine's own agent and for a person or key whose owner holds a member row, false for a superadmin without one. The image route answers 403 on false; the command poll leaves out the signed `screenshot_url`; swoop answers `members_only` at the start and at every lease renewal, which its viewer takes as final, and `capability_missing` to a session read; the dashboard offers neither screenshot nor swoop.
- A talon's image goes only into the email of a recipient who is the site's owner or holds an active member row. Everyone else (the fallback address, a stale `sites[]` entry, a member's cc list, which gets its own copy) gets the verdict and reason without it, and a webhook gets the storage path but never the signed link. A visual check whose author has no member row stops the talon.
- Leaving the site deletes the images under `screenshots/{siteId}/{machineId}/` and the history records, as the last step and best effort, in `removeMachine`, the move's confirm (for the site it left), `deleteSite` and the self-delete of a sole-owner site: a failed delete is logged as an error and never keeps the machine or the site. Clearing a machine's history is different: the person asked for exactly that delete, so a failure throws, the route answers 500 and the history stays.
- A machine's history grows without bound; the screenshot window lists the newest 20.
- The server refuses `start_live_view` and `stop_live_view`. Fielded agents keep the handler until they update; nothing can start it, and a loop already running ends at its own duration, at most 30 minutes.
- Exceptions, by design: an owlette update stays open to a superadmin and runs only an installer owlette publishes: `executeMachineCommand` refuses an `update_owlette` whose `installer_url` and `checksum_sha256` don't match a published file (`isPublishedInstaller`), never a caller-supplied one, and publishing an installer is itself a superadmin action, so that boundary is owlette's release process, not membership. Screenshots a person attaches to an issue are sent to owlette support by that person. A machine granted fleet access (ADR 0017) reads other machines' screenshots through `list_recent_screenshots`, as its grant says, not as a member; writing a grant is a change to fleet settings, so only a member can make one.
